## Purpose

This is the reusable pre-writing evidence layer for the complete `basmi.co.id` catalog. It researches the catalog as one connected pest-management system so articles reuse authoritative sources instead of repeating discovery or drifting into diagnosis, off-label pesticide instructions, fabricated efficacy, or unsafe do-it-yourself procedures.

It is not an article, pest identification, medical or veterinary advice, a pesticide label, a site survey, a treatment plan, legal advice, or proof that a provider, product, or building complies. Writers must cite original sources, not this file.

## How writers must use this file

1. Start with the coverage row for the relevant `BAS-*` family.
2. Open the original source before using a legal, health, pesticide, safety, environmental, efficacy, or performance claim.
3. Treat prevention, survey, identification, exclusion, sanitation, physical control, chemical control, monitoring, and review as distinct linked stages.
4. Never invent an organism identification, infestation severity, disease link, product, dose, mixture, application, re-entry period, treatment frequency, efficacy, warranty, or disposal method.
5. A product registration, label, safety-data sheet, provider credential, service report, and site-specific result are different evidence objects.
6. Preserve stop conditions for suspected poisoning, acute illness, structural damage, fire/electrical hazards, protected wildlife, aggressive stinging insects, and uncontrolled public exposure.
7. Recheck volatile Indonesian rules, registrations, labels, provider status, and local requirements at outline time and before publication.

## Evidence scale

| Grade | Meaning | Permitted use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Current Indonesian law, official registry, or primary public document | Establish public status, jurisdiction, definitions, and visible scope |
| B | WHO/FAO, government, standards-body, or university technical source | Explain mechanisms, control concepts, and evidence methods within stated limits |
| C | Recognized industry or manufacturer guidance | Explain product/system-specific practice without generalizing |
| Gate | Site-, organism-, product-, process-, professional-, or jurisdiction-specific evidence unresolved | Do not publish a definitive diagnosis, number, method, safety, efficacy, legal, or commercial claim |

### KR-02 — Integrated pest/vector management is a decision process

- **Sources:** [WHO vector-control strategy](https://www.who.int/teams/control-of-neglected-tropical-diseases/interventions/strategies/vector-control), [WHO Handbook for Integrated Vector Management](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241502801).
- **Grade:** B.
- **Purpose:** Ground the site's core sequence without reducing IPM to repeated spraying.
- **Summary:** WHO describes integrated vector management as rational decision-making that combines interventions to improve efficacy, cost-effectiveness, ecological soundness, and sustainability.
- **Grounded facts:** Situation analysis, objectives, local ecology, intervention choice, roles, monitoring, evaluation, and adaptation are connected. Chemical control is one possible component, not the definition of control.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-00`, `BAS-01`, `BAS-03`–`BAS-10`, `BAS-13`, `BAS-16`, `BAS-17`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** WHO program guidance is not an Indonesian operator licence, pesticide approval, site plan, disease diagnosis, or product recommendation.

### KR-05 — Pesticide registration and label scope

- **Sources:** [Permentan No. 43 Tahun 2019 — BPK legal record](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/201255/permentan-no-43-), [Kementerian Kesehatan PKRT authorization guidance](https://regalkes.kemkes.go.id/informasi_alkes/014.%20Pedoman%20Pelayanan%20Izin%20Edar%20PKRT%20Bilingual.pdf).

### KR-06 — Pesticide risk management spans the full lifecycle

- **Sources:** [WHO/FAO global pesticide-management survey](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241516884), [WHO 2025 risk-reduction guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240111905).
- **Grade:** B.
- **Purpose:** Connect procurement and use to exposure, storage, transport, resistance, waste, and phase-out decisions.
- **Summary:** WHO/FAO treats registration, procurement, storage, transport, distribution, application, quality, exposure, resistance, and disposal as a management system and recommends reducing reliance on chemical pesticides where feasible.
- **Grounded facts:** Hazard and exposure both matter. “Approved,” “natural,” “low odor,” or “professional grade” alone does not establish safety for a person, animal, surface, or environment.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-13`, `BAS-14`, `BAS-15`, `BAS-16`, `BAS-18`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** International guidance does not override an Indonesian label or emergency instruction.

### KR-07 — Identification and monitoring precede target-specific action

- **Sources:** [CDC Integrated Pest Management](https://www.cdc.gov/nceh/ehs/docs/factsheets/ipm-in-schools.pdf), [US EPA Integrated Pest Management principles](https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/integrated-pest-management-ipm-principles).
- **Grade:** B/C; foreign educational guidance, not Indonesian law.

- **Summary:** IPM begins with correct identification, monitoring, prevention, and action decisions, then evaluates results.
- **Grounded facts:** Signs, specimens, locations, dates, environmental conditions, damage, device data, and trend can support identification and severity assessment; one sign can have multiple causes.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-00`, `BAS-01`, `BAS-02`–`BAS-10`, `BAS-12`, `BAS-13`, `BAS-17`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Obtain local entomology, pest, wildlife, building, or public-health expertise before species, severity, vector, disease, or treatment claims.

### KR-08 — Termite evidence is not structural adequacy

- **Sources:** [US EPA termite control information](https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/termites-how-identify-and-control-them), [USDA Forest Products Laboratory publications](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/research/research_emphasis_areas/wood_protection.php).
- **Grade:** B/C; foreign technical context.
- **Purpose:** Separate termite signs and control pathways from building diagnosis and repair.
- **Summary:** Termite management combines identification, moisture/building conditions, prevention, treatment options, and monitoring; wood damage may require separate structural assessment.
- **Grounded facts:** Swarmers, discarded wings, mud tubes, hollow/damaged wood, moisture, soil contact, and treatment history are evidence inputs, not proof of species or remaining capacity.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-01`, `BAS-02`, `BAS-12`, `BAS-13`, `BAS-14`, and `BAS-17`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No DIY termiticide, drilling, barrier, bait placement, or structural verdict. Require Indonesian product labels, a qualified termite specialist, and an engineer for structural concerns.

### KR-09 — Rodent and food-site control prioritize source, access, and evidence

- **Sources:** [CDC rodent-control guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/rodent-control.html), [Codex General Principles of Food Hygiene](https://www.fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius/sh-proxy/pt/?lnk=1&url=https%253A%252F%252Fworkspace.fao.org%252Fsites%252Fcodex%252FStandards%252FCXC%2B1-1969%252FCXC_001e.pdf).
- **Grade:** B.
- **Purpose:** Ground proofing, sanitation, contamination control, monitoring, and food-facility interfaces.
- **Summary:** Food, water, harborage, entry paths, contamination, exclusion, traps/devices, pesticide controls, carcasses, and monitoring require coordinated management.
- **Grounded facts:** Droppings, gnawing, tracks, odor, sightings, burrows, and device data have different evidential value. Cleanup and food protection require their own exposure controls.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-01`, `BAS-03`, `BAS-12`–`BAS-17`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not diagnose disease, publish bait or trap placement recipes, or infer legal wildlife status. Use the applicable label and health/wildlife authority.

### KR-10 — Cockroach, ant, bed-bug, and fly programs are ecology-specific

- **Sources:** [US EPA cockroach IPM](https://www.epa.gov/ipm/identifying-and-controlling-cockroaches), [US EPA bed bug information](https://www.epa.gov/bedbugs), [CDC bed bug clinical boundary](https://www.cdc.gov/bed-bugs/about/index.html).
- **Grade:** B/C.
- **Purpose:** Prevent one generic insect procedure from being copied across organism families.
- **Summary:** Harborages, food/water, colony behavior, egg/life stages, source material, movement, resistance, and monitoring differ by organism and setting.
- **Grounded facts:** Bites and skin marks do not confirm bed bugs; winged ants can be confused with termites; adult fly capture does not by itself identify a breeding source; pesticide exposure can change behavior and detectability.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-01`, `BAS-04`–`BAS-07`, `BAS-11`–`BAS-17`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Require competent identification and site evidence; no heat, steam, fumigation, pesticide, medical, or eradication recipes.

### KR-11 — Mosquito source reduction and surveillance precede claims about disease control

- **Sources:** [Kemenkes PSN 3M Plus](https://ayosehat.kemkes.go.id/pemberantasan-sarang-nyamuk-dengan-3m-plus), [WHO insecticide-resistance monitoring manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240051089).
- **Grade:** A/B.
- **Purpose:** Ground dengue/mosquito content in Indonesian source reduction, monitoring, and authority coordination.
- **Summary:** Kemenkes promotes draining/cleaning, closing water storage, and reusing/recycling potential containers plus context-appropriate prevention. WHO emphasizes surveillance and resistance evidence when selecting vector interventions.
- **Grounded facts:** Container/source surveys, larvae/adult surveillance, environmental management, barriers, community action, chemical interventions, resistance, and disease surveillance are different layers.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-00`, `BAS-01`, `BAS-08`, `BAS-13`–`BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not declare an outbreak, diagnose dengue, or provide larvicide/fogging doses. Current public-health authorities and product labels control chemical interventions.

### KR-12 — Stinging insects, wildlife, pets, and aquaria create stop conditions

- **Sources:** [WHO animal-bites fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/animal-bites), [Kementerian Lingkungan Hidup dan Kehutanan](https://www.menlhk.go.id/), [Kementerian Kesehatan](https://www.kemkes.go.id/).
- **Grade:** Gate; official doorways establish escalation domains, not species-specific instructions.
- **Purpose:** Prevent removal tutorials, wildlife poisoning, allergy treatment, or “pet-safe” promises.
- **Summary:** Immediate sting/bite risk, vulnerable occupants, nest/location, animal welfare, protected status, disease exposure, pets, aquaria, and chemical sensitivity can require isolation and specialist referral.
- **Grounded facts:** A photograph may not establish species, aggressiveness, protection, disease risk, or safe removal. Aquatic organisms can be exposed through drift, runoff, residues, air handling, and contaminated equipment.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `BAS-01`, `BAS-09`, `BAS-10`, `BAS-13`–`BAS-16`, and `BAS-19`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No nest removal, trapping, poisoning, antidote, diagnosis, or emergency-medication instruction; use emergency, medical, veterinary, wildlife, and licensed pest professionals.

### KR-13 — Cleaning, sanitation, and disinfection are different processes

- **Sources:** [WHO cleaning and disinfection guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-2019-nCoV-Disinfection-2020.1), [Kementerian Kesehatan PKRT authorization guidance](https://regalkes.kemkes.go.id/informasi_alkes/014.%20Pedoman%20Pelayanan%20Izin%20Edar%20PKRT%20Bilingual.pdf).

## Cross-catalog fact bank

1. Integrated management is a decision cycle; it is not a synonym for pesticide application.
2. Identification, infestation severity, health risk, building damage, product selection, and legal compliance are separate conclusions.
3. A registration, label, safety-data sheet, provider credential, service report, and site result prove different things.
4. Chemical controls never enter an article as invented doses, mixtures, placements, frequencies, re-entry periods, first aid, or disposal instructions.
5. Food, water, shelter, access, moisture, and human operations can be measured before and after intervention.
6. Monitoring data need a dated baseline, location/device map, method, threshold/action logic, corrective action, and trend.
7. Fogging, odor, visible mortality, or one quiet visit does not by itself prove source removal, disease prevention, or durable control.
8. “Natural,” “pet-safe,” “child-safe,” “non-toxic,” “eco-friendly,” and “guaranteed” require scoped product, exposure, efficacy, and operator evidence.
9. Pest evidence does not establish structural adequacy, medical diagnosis, wildlife legality, or food/health-facility compliance.
10. Company services, prices, coverage, schedules, credentials, products, cases, and warranties are volatile publication-time evidence.
